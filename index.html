The Mechanics

Why this isn't just another debate app.

Three design decisions separate Brawl from every Q&A forum and debate platform that came before it.

01 / THE HOOK
Zero prep. Three seconds to live.
No research time. No tab-opening. No AI-assisted copy-paste. The prompt drops, the camera goes live in three seconds. The unscripted-ness is the training and the entertainment.
02 / THE STAKE
Votes cost clout. Judging isn't free.
Each viewer gets one vote per Brawl — and voting wagers your own clout. Vote for the winner, you gain a point. Vote for the loser, you lose one. The audience is forced to judge the argument.
03 / THE FLOOR
Lose the brawl, not your dignity.
Every player has a regenerating base rating and a capped loss per match. The downside is a sting, not an eviction. You want the loser furious enough to come back tomorrow.
The Thesis

We're not building a debate app. We're building a status economy.

On every other platform, clout flows to whoever has the most followers, the best camera, the prettiest feed. Brawl rewards a single currency that can't be faked: the quality of a mind under pressure, in sixty seconds, with no preparation.

The quiet kid in the last row — the one with no Instagram following — can dethrone the campus celebrity live, at 9 PM, in front of everyone who matters to them. Not because they're popular. Because they're sharper.

Brawl is the first status economy where intellectual courage outranks followers.

Every feature decision should be testable against it: does this make sharp thinking win? If yes, keep it. If no, it dies.

The campus product is the marketing. The enterprise product — a B2B communication gym where companies pay to make their employees think and speak clearly under pressure — is the business.
